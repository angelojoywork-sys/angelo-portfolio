Cairn Homes is one of Ireland’s leading home and community builders, known for developing sustainable, high-quality residential neighborhoods across the Greater Dublin Area. Hampden Hill in Donabate is one of their flagship coastal residential communities, blending modern energy-efficient architecture with natural landscapes.

I designed promotional press advertisements for new station view apartments in national newspapers (The Irish Times and Irish Independent) as well as on-site signage and hoarding panels for the future school site.

Within a 2 day turnaround, I was accountable for all creative choices, from typography and layout hierarchy to image compositing for Press ads and the on-site signage. The hoarding design required balancing bilingual English and Irish messaging, all while tailoring the overall aesthetic to a demographic of prospective buyers and adhering to Cairn Homes/Hampden Hill brand standards.

I began by studying Cairn Homes’ brand guidelines, analyzing existing national press ads, and researching architectural editorial trends to establish a clean, premium commercial style.

![Press advertisements](01.jpg)

![Press advertisements](02.jpg)

I started designing on InDesign to make the brand stand out, integrating the coastal background texture from the Hampden Hill website to frame the layout. I structured a strict typographic hierarchy using the brand's humanistic sans-serif typeface, followed by hero apartment photography, key messaging, pricing, and mandatory Irish housing disclosures (BER ratings and legal info). Mindful of print aesthetics and business strategy, I intentionally omitted QR codes to preserve a clean reader experience, opting instead for a clear website URL and the corporate tagline ("Built for Good") in a bottom brand band.

![Press ad layout](03.jpg)

For the estate sign, I drew inspiration from existing site collateral to design a balanced color-blocked layout. Rather than using a full-bleed image, I intentionally positioned the photography to create dedicated breathing room for the Hampden Hill logo, headline, body copy, and a closing note.

![Estate sign](04.jpg)

![Hoarding panels](05.jpg)

For the large-format school hoarding panels, I optimized the layout for pedestrian and vehicular visibility by using high-contrast color palettes (alternating light and dark brand tones across the panels), massive typography, and strict spacing. I ensured critical text and logos avoided structural panel joints, headerboards, and kicker plates. Additionally, I managed complex bilingual requirements, collaborating with the client and native Irish speakers to verify accurate Irish translations alongside official Department of Education and Project Ireland 2040 co-branding.

![Used AI for illustrative purposes of the panel mockup](06.jpg)

Technical Production: All large-format assets were built at a 1:10 scale with vector artwork, high-resolution imagery, proper bleed allowances, and correct color profiles to prevent pixelation and ensure seamless physical installation.

The campaign successfully delivered all print assets, including national press ads, estate signage, and large-format school hoarding panels, meeting the tight 2 day turnaround time and earning strong client approval for their clean, premium execution.

This project deepened my technical expertise in print production, specifically mastering the precise color management and image treatment required for high-quality reproduction in The Irish Times and The Irish Independent. For large-format hoarding, I mastered structural best practices, such as setting up Illustrator files at a 1:10 scale with vector artwork, ensuring generous typography sizing and line spacing for distance viewing, and strategically positioning content to avoid structural panel joints.

Additionally, I gained valuable experience handling complex bilingual layouts (English and Irish) and precise corporate logomark placement across multi-format estate collateral.
